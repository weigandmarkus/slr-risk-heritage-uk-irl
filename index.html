About this Project

What is this map?

This interactive tool visualizes the threat of future sea-level rise to the archaeological heritage of the United Kingdom and Ireland. By intersecting advanced climate projections with national heritage registers, we identify which historical sites are at risk of being lost to the ocean under different climate scenarios.

Theoretical Basis: Relative Sea Level (RSL)

This project visualizes Relative Sea Level (RSL) rather than just global averages. RSL accounts for local vertical land motion. In the UK and Ireland, the land in the north is rising (recovering from the last Ice Age), while the south is sinking.
Note: The Shetland Islands are excluded from this model to maintain a higher resolution focus on the contiguous landmasses of Great Britain and Ireland.

Modelling Workflow: Flood Zones

1. DEM Preparation:

  • Source: Derived from Copernicus GLO-30 (DGED), using 32-bit floating-point precision to accurately model decimal water rises.
  • Processing: 89 individual elevation tiles were mosaicked into a single continuous surface and reprojected to Europe Albers Equal Area Conic for accurate area calculation.
  • Resampling: Pixels were resampled to be perfectly square to ensure interpolation algorithms calculated distance accurately.

2. Inundation Analysis (Modified Bathtub Model):

  • Water Surface: We used IDW (Inverse Distance Weighted) Interpolation on regional projection points (derived from the NASA/IPCC Projection Tool). This creates a sloping water surface that accounts for regional gravity and tidal differences.
  • Hydrological Connectivity: To prevent false flooding in isolated inland lowlands (e.g., quarries), we applied a Region Group algorithm. This filtered out isolated "puddles," ensuring only areas physically connected to the ocean are mapped as flooded.

3. Web Optimization:

  • Vector zones were simplified using the Douglas-Peucker algorithm (100m tolerance) and a minimum area threshold of 50,000 m² to optimize performance without losing visual accuracy.

Modelling Workflow: Archaeological Data

1. Data Harmonization:

  • We aggregated data from five separate national agencies (Historic England, HES, Cadw, NMS, and DfC).
  • Because every country uses different naming conventions, we created a unified schema, mapping local fields into a standardized format.

2. The Risk Matrix:

  • Instead of creating separate map layers for every year, we calculated a risk matrix. Every site was spatially intersected with every flood scenario.
  • This resulted in a binary coding system (0 = Safe, 1 = Flooded) embedded in the site's attributes, allowing the web map to instantly filter thousands of sites without reloading data.

Scenario Definitions (IPCC AR6)

Source: IPCC AR6 WGI: Framing, Context, and Methods (2021), Cross-Chapter Box 1.4:

  • SSP1-2.6 (Paris Agreement): Stays below 2.0°C warming relative to 1850-1900 with implied net zero emissions in the second half of the century.
  • SSP2-4.5 (Middle of the Road): Deviates mildly from a ‘no-additional-climate-policy’ reference scenario, resulting in a best-estimate warming around 2.7°C by 2100.
  • SSP5-8.5 (Fossil-Fueled): A high reference scenario with no additional climate policy. Emission levels this high are only associated with fossil-fueled development pathways.

Data Sources

Geospatial & Climate Data

  • Elevation Model: Copernicus GLO-30 (DGED) - European Space Agency.
  • Sea Level Projections: IPCC AR6 (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change).
  • Control Point Data: NASA Sea Level Projection Tool.
  • Basemap: Stadia Maps, OpenMapTiles, OpenStreetMap.

Archaeological Data

  • Ireland: National Monuments Service (Archaeological Survey of Ireland).
  • Scotland: Historic Environment Scotland (Scheduled Monuments).
  • England: Historic England (National Heritage List).
  • Wales: Cadw (Scheduled Monuments).
  • Northern Ireland: Department for Communities (Sites and Monuments Record).
